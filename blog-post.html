Poly-tunnel or custom greenhouse? Start with the crop, not the catalogue

The two structures we build solve different problems. Which one suits you depends on what you intend to grow, the conditions on your site and the scale you are working at.

The question we get asked most often is which type of greenhouse someone should build. It is the right question, but it is usually asked in the wrong order — structure first, crop second. We would rather work the other way round.

We build two things: poly-tunnel systems, and custom greenhouse systems designed for particular climates and crops. Both are genuinely useful. Neither is automatically the better choice, and the honest answer to "which should I get?" is that we cannot tell you until we know what you are growing and where.

What a poly-tunnel is good at

A tunnel is the practical starting point for most growers moving from open field cultivation to protected growing. It gets a crop under cover at a workable scale without the complexity of a bespoke build.

Because the format is well understood, we can specify the things that go inside it at the same time — the irrigation layout, whether you are growing hydroponically, and which coco peat format suits the rows. That matters more than people expect. A tunnel with mismatched irrigation is a tunnel you will be fighting for years.

  • Covered growing at commercial scale
  • Irrigation and hydroponic systems fitted to the layout
  • Grow bags, slabs and planter bags supplied to suit the rows

When a custom structure earns its cost

Sometimes the crop, the site or the climate asks for something a standard tunnel does not give. That is when we design the structure around the requirement rather than fitting the requirement to a structure.

This is where greenhouse consultancy does most of its work — deciding what the building actually has to achieve before anything is priced or built. It is slower at the start and cheaper in the end.

A structure is only right if the crop, the climate and the system inside it were considered together.

The four things that decide it

Whichever way a project goes, the same four inputs shape the design. None of them is about the building itself.

  1. Your crop. How it is planted, supported and handled, and what its roots need.
  2. Your climate. The conditions your site actually experiences, not regional averages.
  3. Your site and scale. Space, access and project size, which drive both design and cost.
  4. Your system. The irrigation, hydroponics and growing media that will live inside it.

Why there is no price list

People sometimes read the absence of published prices as evasiveness. It is the opposite. The same crop at two different scales is two different projects, and quoting an average would mean quoting something that is wrong for almost everyone.

What to send us The crop, the site and the scale you have in mind. That is genuinely enough to start — we will ask about the rest once we understand the shape of it.

If you are weighing this up for your own operation, message us on WhatsApp or send the details through. We will tell you which of the two we would actually recommend, and why — before anyone talks about money.
